This update incorporates older records newly located for the project.
It does not announce a new excavation, a site inspection, or a deed
resolving the lordship's descent. Published heritage summaries have
been read; the original reports identified through them remain
uninspected and are marked accordingly in the
[research ledger]({{ '/research' | relative_url }}).

## Castle, Drain, and Garden

The [Norman chapter]({{ '/history/norman-period#excavated-bailey' | relative_url }})
now includes the 1981 excavation at 15a Dane John and the 1996 mound
survey, using [Kent HER's ditch](https://heritage.kent.gov.uk/Monument/MKE93130/)
and [mound records](https://heritage.kent.gov.uk/Monument/MKE77155).
The references to Rady's 1982 report and Jarman's article, catalogued
as published in 1999, make the next inspection concrete. The dates
of fieldwork and publication are kept separate.

## Mansion and Outfarm

[What Survives of the Dungeon?]({{ '/history/surviving-estate#mansion-and-building-reports' | relative_url }})
adds the architectural source trail from
[MKE24851](https://heritage.kent.gov.uk/Monument/MKE24851): the CAT
Annual Report 1977–78, page 12, and City Council/RCHME survey 57025.
The nearby demolished outfarm in
[MKE86264](https://heritage.kent.gov.uk/Monument/MKE86264) is a separate
mapping lead, not an established part of the manor.

## Corrections and an Unresolved Date

The [Roman chapter]({{ '/history/roman-origins' | relative_url }}) no
longer presents the mound as an established Roman military lookout
or asserts Anglo-Saxon defensive reuse. The Norman chapter no longer
assumes an unchanged prehistoric earthwork. These changes extend the
qualified chronology recorded in the
[October note]({{ '/research/notes/2026-10-12-place-and-evidence' | relative_url }}),
rather than replacing it with another certain origin story.

The [gardens page]({{ '/gardens#monument-date' | relative_url }}) now
records a conflict within Historic England's descriptions: the
[gardens entry](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1001360)
gives 1803 for the summit monument, while the
[pinnacle listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1085047)
gives 1830. The former date is retained provisionally pending
contemporary evidence. Existing dated research notes, modern title
instruments, and the assessment of the later succession are unchanged.
