Why this site exists

An irresistible name became a serious historical project.

In March 2024, James P. Howard, II, paid £175 for documents purporting to convey the possessory title “Lord of the Manor of the Dungeon.” The name was irresistible. The seller's chain to the historical lordship was not.

Instead of asking the paperwork to carry more weight than it could, the purchase became the starting point for this public history: an expanding archive of the place, its people, and the evidence that connects them. The modern documents are published in full, with their limitations stated just as plainly as their claims.
