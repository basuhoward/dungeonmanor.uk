The mansion's demolition is not the end of the estate's architectural
history. Historic England identifies **Dane John Works, 45 Gordon Road**,
as a surviving mid-sixteenth-century building of the Dane John Manor
estate. It is Grade II listed, entry **1273536**. The
[official listing and location map](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1273536)
provide a fixed point for investigating the buildings outside the city
walls, distinct from the [public gardens]({{ '/gardens' | relative_url }})
within them.

## The Building Recorded in the Listing

The description records sixteenth-century brickwork and stone dressings
beneath later rendering, surviving oak floor timbers and roof construction,
and a pair of nineteenth-century square oasts. It describes the property
as the remaining estate building, rather than an intact survival of the
whole mansion. Its national grid reference is **TR 14790 57185**.

Those details offer questions for further building research. Which parts
belong to the earliest structure? How did its use change as additions were
made? How does its footprint relate to the buildings shown on estate and
railway plans? The listing is a starting point for that work, not a
substitute for examining the fabric or the relevant plans.

## Two Different Demolition Accounts

The site's [Hasted archive entry]({{ '/archives/hasted-history-of-kent' | relative_url }})
records Thomas Lee Warner's demolition of the mansion in **1752**, with
some offices, garden walls, and the adjacent farmhouse and buildings left
standing. The underlying account is Hasted's
[“Canterbury: Manors,” volume 11, pages 147–164](https://www.british-history.ac.uk/survey-kent/vol11/pp147-164).
Historic England's building description, by contrast, dates the clearance
of other estate buildings for the railway to **1859**.

These accounts should not be compressed into one demolition date. Read
together, they suggest a sequence in which the mansion disappeared first
and further buildings were cleared a century later. Establishing exactly
which structure was removed at each stage still requires plans and
building-specific evidence. The listing does not, on its own, identify
Dane John Works with a particular room or office mentioned by Hasted.

## A Building Is Not a Chain of Title

This survival makes the estate less abstract, but it does not resolve the
[Lee-Warner succession]({{ '/history/lee-warner-era' | relative_url }}).
A building history and a lordship history ask different questions. The
building's identification cannot establish who inherited or acquired the
separate lordship. The [research ledger]({{ '/research' | relative_url }})
keeps the documentary and topographical inquiries connected without
allowing one to stand in for the other.

This page is based on the published listing, not a new site inspection.
It does not establish public access to the building or its interior.
