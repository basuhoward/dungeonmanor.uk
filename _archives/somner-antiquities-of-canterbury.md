William Somner (1598-1669) was a Canterbury-born scholar who served as
Registrar of the Ecclesiastical Court of Canterbury. His _Antiquities of
Canterbury_, first published in 1640, was the first systematic scholarly
treatment of the city's Roman, Saxon, and medieval history and remained
the foundation of Canterbury antiquarianism for more than two centuries.
A second edition prepared by Nicholas Battely was published in 1703 and
is the standard scholarly reference.

## Significance for the Dungeon

Godfrey-Faussett drew on Somner and Battely when tracing the history of
the Dane John mounds in his 1875 paper. He noted that the plan of Canterbury
in Somner's 1640 edition depicted the mounds in a conventionalized manner,
and that the Battely edition preserved valuable material on the city's
topographic and nomenclatural history. Later writers on the etymology of
"Dane John" and its relationship to the Norman French _donjon_ also drew
on Somner and Battely as foundational references.
