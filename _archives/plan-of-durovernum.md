This black-and-white plan of Durovernum—Roman Canterbury—was reproduced in
Charles Cotton's 1929 study of the Saxon cathedral. It is a schematic
historical reconstruction, not an archaeological survey or a pictorial view.

The plan marks the conjectured Roman walls and street grid, the River Stour,
gates, and principal public buildings. It is useful as evidence of how
scholars in the early twentieth century understood the Roman city. Its labels
and identifications should be checked against later archaeological work before
being treated as current conclusions.

Cotton, Charles. _[The Saxon Cathedral at Canterbury and the Saxon Saints
Buried Therein]({{ '/archives/the-saxon-cathedral-at-canterbury-and-the-saxon-saints-buried-therein' | relative_url }})_. Manchester: Manchester University Press, 1929.
