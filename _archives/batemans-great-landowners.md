John Bateman's _Great Landowners_, first issued in 1876 and repeatedly
revised, distilled the parliamentary Return of Owners of Land of 1873
into the standard reference on who owned Victorian Britain. Bateman
listed every owner of substantial acreage with their seat and holdings,
and his pages remain the quickest way to place a landed family at a
fixed point in the late nineteenth century.

## The Lee-Warners in Bateman

The 1878 edition records, at page 466, both branches of the family that
had held the Manor of the Dungeon since 1680: "LEE-WARNER, Henry James,
of Walsingham Abbey, Fakenham," the senior line in Norfolk, and
"LEE-WARNER, Robert Henry, of Tibberton Court, Hereford," the branch
descended from the Reverend Daniel Henry Lee Warner, with which the
evidence places the Canterbury property. The entry fixes Robert Henry
Lee-Warner at Tyberton in the year of Bateman's survey, consistent
with the census returns of 1881 and 1891 and with his presumptive
tenure of the manor between 1858 and his death in 1895.
