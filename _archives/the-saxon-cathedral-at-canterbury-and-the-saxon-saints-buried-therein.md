Charles Cotton's study reconstructs the Saxon cathedral at Canterbury and
catalogues the saints associated with it. Written for archaeological-society
members and general readers, it follows the church from Augustine's mission
through the Norman rebuilding under Lanfranc, drawing on documentary and
architectural scholarship available in the early twentieth century.

## Relevance to the Dungeon

The book's principal subject is Canterbury Cathedral rather than the Manor of
the Dungeon. It belongs in this archive because its plan of Roman Durovernum
provides a useful comparison with Godfrey-Faussett's 1875 maps and because the
cathedral's changing precinct helps establish the wider topography within which
the mound and later manor developed.

Cotton's conclusions reflect scholarship published in 1929. The volume is best
used as a historical synthesis and a record of its period's reconstruction,
not as a substitute for current archaeological assessments.
