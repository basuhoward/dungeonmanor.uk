William Henry Ireland (1777-1835), a figure better remembered for his
youthful Shakespeare forgeries than for his later, entirely respectable
topographical labors, compiled a four-volume history of Kent published
between 1828 and 1830, with engraved views by George Shepherd and Henry
Gastineau. The work leans heavily on Hasted, as every Kent history of
the period did, but its value for the Manor of the Dungeon lies
precisely in what it updates.

## The Manor in the 1820s

Ireland's first volume carries the manor's story a generation past
Hasted. It confirms the descent through the Hales, Lee, and Lee Warner
families, and records that the manorial estate in the parish of St.
Mary Bredin was "now known by the name of Deanjohn Farm." This is the
earliest identification of the manor's surviving physical form as a
named working farm, the thread by which the lordship can be followed
through the nineteenth-century record: the farm appears under the name
Donjon Farm in the Tyberton Court muniments, and under the name Dane
John Farm in Canterbury records as late as 1875.
