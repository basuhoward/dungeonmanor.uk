Alongside his twelve-volume survey of Kent, Edward Hasted published a
dedicated two-volume history of Canterbury in 1801, treating the city's
civil and ecclesiastical history at a depth the county survey could not
accommodate. For the Manor of the Dungeon it contains two passages of
particular importance.

## The Manor and Its Advowson

At page 221 of the first volume, Hasted describes the state of the
manor at the close of the eighteenth century: the mansion, "situated
just without the city walls, at a small distance westward from the
lesser hill of the Dungeon, in the parish of St. Mary Bredin, has been
pulled down for some years, and only part of the out-offices are
remaining, with part of the garden walls."

At page 313, in his account of St. Mary Bredin church, he records that
the patronage of the church was granted in the thirty-eighth year of
Henry VIII to "the Hales's, lords of the manor of the Dungeon, whose
burial place was within this church; since which the patronage of it
has continued in the possession of the owners of that manor." This
sentence is the linchpin of the manor's later history: it establishes
the advowson of St. Mary Bredin as a proxy for the lordship itself, so
that the recorded patrons and presentations of the parish trace the
manor's owners through periods when no deed of the manor survives in
print.
